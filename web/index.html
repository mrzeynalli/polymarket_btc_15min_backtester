Backtest a strategy

Buy a side once it reaches a price, then hold to settlement — or sell if it falls back to a stop loss, which you can arm only from a chosen minute. Every order is executed against the recorded Level-2 book with latency, so it can fill partially or not at all.

Measured on the ask — the price you would actually pay, not the midpoint. This is the trigger, not necessarily the price you get.
The highest ask the order will accept. It matters more than it looks, because the trigger above only says when to buy, not at what price:
  • If the ask is already past your trigger when the window opens — normal when the window starts late — you buy at whatever it is now, up to this ceiling.
  • If the book moves while your order is in flight, anything above this is abandoned instead of filled.
A binary bought at p has to settle in your favour p of the time just to break even, so a loose ceiling quietly raises the bar. Keep it close to the trigger — 0.81 for a 0.80 trigger — unless you deliberately want to chase.

About this platform

This site records every Polymarket BTC UP/DOWN 15-minute market straight off the public CLOB WebSocket feed — full Level-2 order books, trades, and the Binance/Chainlink BTC reference price — and normalizes the raw messages into structured, decimal-clean datasets. History replays that data as price charts and reconstructed order books; Backtest replays it as fills, running a strategy against the same recorded books. Nothing here is a wallet, and nothing here submits an order.

Markets recorded—15-minute UP/DOWN intervals

How it works
Raw feed → normalized data → charts and backtests
01

Collected live over WebSocket

A collector subscribes to the public CLOB market-data socket for every BTC UP/DOWN market the moment it opens, and keeps the full, unfiltered stream — snapshots, level updates, and trades — with nothing summarized away.

02

Normalized to fixed-point, decimal-clean data

Raw messages are parsed into typed tables: order-book snapshots, level updates, trades, and BTC reference prices, all at exact fixed-point scale so nothing is silently rounded through floating point.

03

Visualized as history

The History view turns that normalized data into UP/DOWN price paths, spreads, and a full reconstructed order book at any instant you pick.

04

Replayable for backtesting

The Backtest view runs a strategy — entry price, stop loss, position size, staking — against every recorded market by walking the real recorded book, so fills reflect what the book could actually have given you.
